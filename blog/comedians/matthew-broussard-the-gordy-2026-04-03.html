Matthew Broussard Brings His Heady Humor to The Gordy in Houston

Matthew Broussard has a knack for transforming the seemingly mundane into a cerebral comedy experience. Whether he's dissecting the etymology of pharmaceutical names or delving into the peculiarities of millennial masculinity, Broussard’s stand-up is a masterclass in turning personal quirks and intellectual curiosities into punchlines. As he prepares to take the stage at The Gordy in Houston on April 3, 2026, audiences can expect an evening that’s as thought-provoking as it is hilarious.

Broussard's comedic journey began in 2011, right here in Houston, while he was still crunching numbers as a financial analyst. With a degree in computational mathematics from Rice University, his analytical background informs a style that is both polished and precisely timed. His debut special, "Hyperbolic," released in December 2024, showcases his ability to weave personal narratives with broader observations, available everywhere comedy is streamed or sold. This meticulous approach has landed him appearances on "The Tonight Show" and "Conan," where his heady, self-effacing humor resonates with audiences.

His comedy is as much about the punchlines as it is about the insights. Broussard's routines often explore his Cajun-Jewish upbringing in a Southern prep school, the dynamics of being a "bread loser" to a former pro athlete, and candid tales of familial mental health struggles. His delivery is deliberate, each joke a carefully crafted piece of a larger, intricate puzzle. It's a style that feels as much like a lecture on the absurdities of life as it does a stand-up set, with topics ranging from grammar and statistics to the more personal intricacies of his family life.

Live, Broussard is known for maintaining an engaging energy that keeps the room buzzing. His performances at venues like The Comedy Central Half Hour are marked by a seamless blend of crowd interaction and structured material. At The Gordy, expect a show that’s intimate yet expansive, where every seat feels like the best in the house. The pacing is brisk, the laughs are steady, and the insights, as always, are sharp.

The show at The Gordy, priced between $34.61 and $171.84, promises to be an evening where comedy meets contemplation. As Broussard returns to his Houston roots, the audience will witness firsthand why his blend of humor and intellect has made him a standout in the comedy circuit.
